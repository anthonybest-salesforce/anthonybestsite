The solid face tax
The Newport's milled face is unforgiving by design. Catch the center — perfect. Miss by 4–5mm and the face twists, costing both distance and direction. That's not a flaw in the putter. It's the nature of every solid-face blade, and it demands a consistent center strike every single time.
What playing twice a week means for a blade
Putting touch is a perishable skill. Without practice reps between rounds, your feel for center contact resets. Research shows even good putters miss the sweet spot on roughly 30% of attempts when in form — and that number climbs when you haven't putted in days. The Newport punishes every one of those misses.
The Newport demands the consistency you can't guarantee
The Special Select rewards players who putt frequently enough to keep their strike pattern sharp. For someone who plays two days a week without dedicated practice, the blade's narrow forgiveness window becomes a problem. Your stroke is good. Your equipment is working against it.
The case for replacement: The Newport hasn't broken. But it's built for golfers who practice enough to guarantee center contact. You don't — and you're leaving putts on the table because of it. The three Phantom finalists match your stroke exactly and give you the forgiveness a twice-a-week player deserves.

09 · Neck Architecture
The plumbing neck is a non-negotiable match.
The hosel-to-shaft connection governs toe hang and face balance directly. A neck mismatch forces manipulation through impact — the single most common root cause of directional inconsistency in skilled players.
Plumbing Neck ✓
Shaft runs straight through hosel top. Near-zero offset. Low toe hang. Direct continuation of your current geometry — all three finalists use this architecture.
Slant Neck ✗
Moderate offset and medium toe hang. Requires a slight arc stroke — a mismatch for your profile.
Flow Neck ✗
Full-shaft offset. Higher toe hang. Best for strong-arc strokes — the opposite of your technique.
Short Slant ✗
Compact hosel with light offset. Variable face balance — inconsistent fit profile.

15 · What to Expect
The first few rounds with a new putter.
Three brief adjustments. None of them last more than a few rounds. All of them are worth it.
"The adjustment period is short. The gain — on every round you play for the next several years — is not."
Visual · A few holes
Getting comfortable with a wider head
All three Phantoms are wider than the Newport. Some blade players take a few holes to stop thinking about the size — then never think about it again.
What to do15 minutes on the practice green before your first competitive round. Most players stop noticing the head size by hole 3.
Feel · 3–5 rounds
The insert sounds and feels softer than the Newport
The Newport's solid face is sharper-sounding at impact. The Phantom's Carbon Steel insert is softer. A few players initially read this as less precise feedback — it isn't.
What to doGive it 3–5 rounds. The insert feel becomes your new reference quickly. The distance control is equivalent — the sound is different, not the information.
Tempo · 1–2 rounds
A slightly heavier feel through impact
The wider, heavier head resists rotation slightly more than the Newport. Some players briefly over-steer in the first round or two. It self-corrects quickly.
What to doYou're not changing your stroke — you're learning the putter needs a little less input. Most find this actually simplifies their putting.
